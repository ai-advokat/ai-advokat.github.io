Abstract

The criminal case publicly known as “Chain” provides a significant case study for contemporary North Macedonian criminal law and criminal procedure. Its legal relevance lies not only in the scale of the prosecutorial allegations, the number of natural and legal persons involved, and the alleged loss to the State Budget, but also in the different procedural routes through which individual criminal liability has been, or continues to be, determined: plea agreements concluded before the main hearing, guilty pleas entered during trial, and ordinary evidential proceedings against the remaining defendants. The central thesis is that plea bargaining and guilty pleas are legitimate and necessary instruments of modern criminal procedure, but their use in organised, high-value financial crime must be accompanied by enhanced safeguards of legality, voluntariness, proportionality, individualisation, confiscation, judicial scrutiny and transparent reasoning.
