Abstract

This paper examines electronic and AI-generated evidence as a distinct evidential phenomenon that is reshaping the factual, technical and procedural foundations of judicial decision-making. Its central thesis is that electronic form does not, in itself, diminish evidential value, but it does require verifiable safeguards concerning provenance, integrity, context and the manner of acquisition. With AI-generated and synthetic content, traditional visual or auditory persuasiveness is no longer sufficient. Courts must distinguish admissibility, authenticity, integrity, reliability and evidential weight, while a party must have a genuine opportunity to examine the methodology, data, tool version, error rate and chain of custody. The practical contribution consists of a five-stage test for judicial assessment, a chain-of-custody model, a protocol for suspected deepfakes, questions for expert witnesses and checklists for lawyers and courts.
