Abstract

This paper examines the use of artificial intelligence in legal practice and the judiciary as a question of professional responsibility, the law of evidence and fundamental procedural safeguards. Its central thesis is that AI can materially improve legal research, case organisation, evidential analysis and access to justice, but only where it remains subject to meaningful human control, verifiable sources, documented use and clearly allocated responsibility. Particular attention is given to automated decision-making, algorithmic bias, generative errors, deepfake content, chain of custody, expert evidence, access to the model and liability for damage. The paper concludes by proposing a functional test for permissible use: lawful purpose, necessity, data quality, explainability, human oversight, effective contestability and an individualised decision.
